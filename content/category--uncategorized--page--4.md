<article aria-label="Important methodological updates for Lead-DBS" class="post-item"><div class="archive-media"><a aria-label="Important methodological updates for Lead-DBS" class="archive-image" href="@root@crucial-methodological-updates-for-lead-dbs/" title="1-s2.0-S221315821830305X-gr1-1"><netstim-image data='{"src":"media/9e707b1b9296-1-s2.0-S221315821830305X-gr1-1-card.webp","original":"https://www.netstim.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/1-s2.0-S221315821830305X-gr1-1-180x180.jpg","alt":"","width":180}'/></a></div><div class="standard-content"><header aria-label="Post: Important methodological updates for Lead-DBS"><h2><a href="@root@crucial-methodological-updates-for-lead-dbs/" title="Permanent Link: Important methodological updates for Lead-DBS">Important methodological updates for Lead-DBS</a></h2><span class="post-meta"><time datetime="2018-11-04T18:05:24+00:00">November 4, 2018</time>/<a href="@root@crucial-methodological-updates-for-lead-dbs/#respond">0 Comments</a>/in <a href="@root@category/uncategorized/">Uncategorized</a>/by <a href="@root@author/andreashorn/">andreashorn</a></span></header><p>We are thrilled that the <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327380509_Lead-DBS_v2_Towards_a_comprehensive_pipeline_for_deep_brain_stimulation_imaging">Lead-DBS v2 methodological paper</a> is out in NeuroImage.</p>
<p>[An unpaywalled preprint can be found <a href="https://www.biorxiv.org/content/early/2018/08/28/322008">here</a>].</p>

<p>Lead-DBS was developed since 2012 and the first methods paper published in 2014. Since then, nearly everything has changed – and this led us to writing up the aforementioned updated methods paper. In the manuscript, we describe tools for multispectral nonlinear registration, structural/functional connectivity analyses, brain shift correction, reconstruction of microelectrode recordings and orientation detection of segmented DBS leads. The long author list reflects how many people have contributed to Lead-DBS development since then.</p>

<p>One particularly important processing step is nonlinear registration (patient brains to an atlas or the other way around). Currently, ten approaches to do so are readily built into Lead-DBS and each comes with adjustable settings. Thus, it became very important to investigate and quantify results that may originate from those different approaches. In a marathon effort spearheaded by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Siobhan_Ewert">Siobhan Ewert</a> and led by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Todd_Herrington">Todd M Herrington</a> that involved &gt;11,000 nonlinear warps, the two most promising normalization approaches were identified – ANTs based multispectral SyN registration as well as multispectram SPM-based segmentation – which are now the default choices in Lead-DBS. In fact, automatic segmentations of the STN/GPi performed using those approaches were marginally if not at all significantly worse than manual expert segmentations.</p>
<p>Their article <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327908494_Optimization_and_comparative_evaluation_of_nonlinear_deformation_algorithms_for_atlas-based_segmentation_of_DBS_target_nuclei">can be found here</a>.</p>


<p>Finally, a new study out of Bern/Oxford validated the default STN atlas defined in Lead-DBS (<a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317032527_Toward_defining_deep_brain_stimulation_targets_in_MNI_space_A_subcortical_atlas_based_on_multimodal_MRI_histology_and_structural_connectivity">Siobhan Ewert’s DISTAL atlas</a>) using microelectrode recordings. This important work showed that anatomical and electrophysiological accuracy was highest for the DISTAL atlas when comparing it to two different atlases of the STN. <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327921854_Accuracy_of_different_three-dimensional_subcortical_human_brain_atlases_for_DBS_-lead_localisation">The article can be found here</a>.</p>

<p>“<a href="https://www.researchgate.net/deref/http%3A%2F%2Fwww.mdpi.com%2Fjournal%2Fbrainsci%2Fspecial_issues%2FDeep_Brain_Stimulation">Modulatory Effects of Deep Brain Stimulation on Distributed Brain Networks</a>“.</p>

<p>In case you are working on topics that combine brain stimulation with connectomics, would like to write a review or short report in that domain, please consider submitting to this issue – deadline is july 2019.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.researchgate.net/deref/http%3A%2F%2Fwww.mdpi.com%2Fjournal%2Fbrainsci%2Fspecial_issues%2FDeep_Brain_Stimulation" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">http://www.mdpi.com/journal/brainsci/special_issues/Deep_Brain_Stimulation</a></p>

<p>Dear Colleagues,</p>

<p>The field of deep brain stimulation (DBS) is currently experiencing a paradigm-shift, from studying impacts on local brain tissue toward the analysis of modulatory effects on distributed brain networks. With the rise of modern non-invasive neuroimaging methods, this local-to-global shift bears promising potential to better-understand brain disorders, brain function, and to translate novel concepts into clinical practice. Already, network-based targets that may guide surgical planning and DBS programming are being introduced and prospectively validated with the potential to revolutionize the field.</p>
<p>In this Special Issue, we will discuss the indications, potentials, efficacy and validation of network-based brain stimulation concepts with a special focus on DBS. These include the combination of DBS imaging with resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging, diffusion-weighted imaging based tractography and electrophysiological measures such as electroencephalography, magnetoencephalography and local field potential recordings.</p>

Their optimal normalization strategy has now become the default preset of Lead-DBS.</p>

Validation That Subgenual Connectivity Predicts …</a>) spearheaded by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Anne_Weigand">Anne Weigand</a> and led by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Michael_Fox11">Michael D Fox</a>, we were able to explain TMS treatment response by functional connectivity between the stimulation site in the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and the subgenual cingulate cortex. Specifically, strength of anticorrelations between DLPFC and subgenual were predictive of beneficial outcome. This relationship had been hypothesized by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Michael_Fox11">Michael D Fox</a> and colleagues in 2012 (<a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225094403_Efficacy_of_Transcranial_Magnetic_Stimulation_Targets_for_Depression_Is_Related_to_Intrinsic_Functional_Connectivity_with_the_Subgenual_Cingulate">Efficacy of Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation Targets for De…</a>) and was now prospectively validated in a sample of 25 patients treated at BIDMC with TMS.</p>
<p>Moreover, to exclude sham effects, a cohort of 16 patients treated by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Stephan_Taylor">Stephan F Taylor</a> in Michigan was retrospectively analyzed and showed the same relationship.</p>
</div></article>

<p>Inside a new version of Lead-DBS (v2.0.0.6) released yesterday, we included the pallidal theta activity sweet spot defined in <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/321013451_A_localized_pallidal_physiomarker_in_cervical_dystonia">A localized pallidal physiomarker in cervical dystonia </a>as well as optimal stimulation targets defined in <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Thomas_Schoenecker">Thomas Schönecker</a>‘s <span class="publication-type">Article</span> <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266026179_Postoperative_MRI_localisation_of_electrodes_and_clinical_efficacy_of_pallidal_deep_brain_stimulation_in_cervical_dystonia">Postoperative MRI localisation of electrodes and clinical ef… </a>and Philip Starr’s <span class="publication-type">Article</span> <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7159920_Microelectrode-guided_implantation_of_deep_brain_stimulators_into_the_globus_pallidus_internus_for_dystonia_Techniques_electrode_locations_and_outcomes">Microelectrode-guided implantation of deep brain stimulators…</a>.</p>
</p>
<p> Since all three spots fall into a very similar region of the GPi, this atlas gives some summarizing evidence for an optimal target inside the GPi. Of note, the target falls into the sensorimotor part of the GPi as defined in</p>
<p>
<a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317032527_Toward_defining_deep_brain_stimulation_targets_in_MNI_space_A_subcortical_atlas_based_on_multimodal_MRI_histology_and_structural_connectivity">Toward defining deep brain stimulation targets in MNI space:…</a> by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Siobhan_Ewert">Siobhan Ewert</a> et al.
</p>
<p>
<p>We further included the CIT168 subcortical atlas defined in <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320756817_A_High-Resolution_Probabilistic_In_Vivo_Atlas_of_Human_Subcortical_Brain_Nuclei">A High-Resolution Probabilistic In Vivo Atlas of Human Subco… </a>that Wolfgang Pauli and colleagues from Caltech graciously shared for inclusion in Lead-DBS.</p>
</p>
<p>Furthermore, the new Schäfer parcellations defined in <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320255795_Local-Global_Parcellation_of_the_Human_Cerebral_Cortex_from_Intrinsic_Functional_Connectivity_MRI">Local-Global Parcellation of the Human Cerebral Cortex from … </a>that further subdivide the famous 7/17 rs-fMRI brain parcellation established by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/BT_Thomas_Yeo">B.T. Thomas Yeo</a> et al in <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51201884_The_organization_of_the_human_cerebral_cortex_estimated_by_functional_correlation">The organization of the human cerebral cortex estimated by f… </a> have been added for DBS connectivity analyses.</p>

<p>Last but not least, we included <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/deref/http%3A%2F%2Flrnlab.org" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">lrnlab.org</a>‘s (PIs Stephen Coombes &amp; David Villaincourt) HMAT</p>
<p>
<a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7208704_Three-dimensional_locations_and_boundaries_of_motor_and_premotor_cortices_as_defined_by_functional_brain_imaging_A_meta-analysis">Three-dimensional locations and boundaries of motor and prem…</a> and S-MATT <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315113494_A_Template_and_Probabilistic_Atlas_of_the_Human_Sensorimotor_Tracts_using_Diffusion_MRI">A Template and Probabilistic Atlas of the Human Sensorimotor… </a> templates of the sensorimotor cortex and -tract as brain parcellations for connectivity analyses in Lead-DBS and Lead-Connectome (for more information on these very helpful templates see <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/deref/http%3A%2F%2Flrnlab.org%2F" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">http://lrnlab.org/</a>).
</p>
