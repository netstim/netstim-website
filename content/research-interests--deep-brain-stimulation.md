<p>Deep brain stimulation (DBS) uses implanted electrodes to deliver electrical pulses to specific brain regions. Our lab studies how electrode placement and brain connectivity relate to changes in symptoms, especially in Parkinson’s disease, essential tremor and dystonia.</p>

<div class="image-block"><netstim-image data='{"src":"media/c7d2a933fc3c-abb_1-1500x861.png","original":"https://www.netstim.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/abb_1-1030x592.png","alt":"","width":1030}'/></div>

## Lead-DBS

We develop [Lead-DBS](https://netstim.github.io/lead-dbs-website/), an open-source toolbox for locating implanted electrodes on postoperative scans and studying how stimulation sites connect to the rest of the brain.

<div class="action-row"><a class="action external" href="https://netstim.github.io/lead-dbs-website/download/">Download Lead-DBS</a></div>

## Brain atlases

Use [subcortical atlases](https://netstim.github.io/lead-dbs-website/helpsupport/knowledge-base/atlasesresources/atlases-2/) to identify structures near an electrode, or [cortical and whole-brain parcellations](https://netstim.github.io/lead-dbs-website/helpsupport/knowledge-base/atlasesresources/cortical-atlas-parcellations-mni-space/) to study connections across brain regions.

## Lead-DBS methods

- [Subcortical electrophysiology mapping](https://netstim.github.io/lead-dbs-website/helpsupport/knowledge-base/lead-dbs-methods/subcortical-electrophysiology-mapping-sem/) places recordings from different patients in a common anatomical space. [Horn et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.23594) described the method. [Neumann et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.25095) applied it to dystonia.
- [AC/PC to MNI conversion](https://netstim.github.io/lead-dbs-website/helpsupport/knowledge-base/lead-dbs-methods/acpc-to-mni-conversion-2/) translates surgical coordinates into a common reference space. [Horn et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28163141/)
- [DBS network mapping](https://netstim.github.io/lead-dbs-website/helpsupport/knowledge-base/lead-dbs-methods/connectivity-benefit-mapping/) relates a stimulation site’s connections to symptom change. [Horn et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.24974) used this approach to predict outcomes in Parkinson’s disease.

## Project history

<details class="article-disclosure">
<summary>Awards and earlier releases</summary>
<h3>Early recognition</h3>

<p>In 2015, Lead-DBS was awarded with the <a href="https://www.google.de/search?q=max+rubner+preis+charite+horn&amp;oq=max+rubn&amp;aqs=chrome.0.69i59j69i57j69i60l4.1653j0j1&amp;sourceid=chrome&amp;ie=UTF-8"><strong>Max-Rubner-Prize</strong> for Innovation</a> by <a href="http://www.stiftung-charite.de/">Stiftung Charité</a>, in 2016 a <strong><a href="https://www.radcliffe.harvard.edu/exploratory-seminars/toward-connectomic-deep-brain-stimulation">Harvard Radcliffe Exploratory Ventures Grant</a></strong> by the <a href="https://www.radcliffe.harvard.edu/">Harvard Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study</a>. In 2017, Lead-DBS was awarded the <strong>Robert-Koch-Prize</strong> of Charité, Berlin.</p>

<p>By 2023, researchers had downloaded Lead-DBS over 65,000 times, and groups from over 20 institutions had contributed code and content.</p>
