## DEEP BRAIN STIMULATION

The main focus of our work is **deep brain stimulation** (DBS) – predominantly in movement disorders such as Parkinson’s Disease, Essential Tremor or Dystonia – more rarely in cognitive disorders such as Depression or Alzheimer’s Disease. In DBS, electrodes are **surgically implanted** into deep (subcortical) areas of the brain and – using an implanted impulse generator – these structures are **stimulated chronically**. The figure below shows a reconstruction of **two DBS leads implanted with target subthalamic nucleus** (orange) to treat **Parkinson’s** **Disease.**

## Building tools to reconstruct electrode placement

Since 2012, we develop an open-source toolbox to facilitate reconstruction of DBS electrode placement based on postoperative imaging.

**Lead-DBS** has since become a leading platform for **DBS imaging research** with specific focus on **brain connectivity** between DBS stimulation sites and other areas in the brain.

## Impact of Lead-DBS

In 2015, [Lead-DBS](http://lead-dbs.org/) was awarded with the [**Max-Rubner-Prize** for Innovation](https://www.google.de/search?q=max+rubner+preis+charite+horn&oq=max+rubn&aqs=chrome.0.69i59j69i57j69i60l4.1653j0j1&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8) by [Stiftung Charité](http://www.stiftung-charite.de/), in 2016 a **[Harvard Radcliffe Exploratory Ventures Grant](https://www.radcliffe.harvard.edu/exploratory-seminars/toward-connectomic-deep-brain-stimulation)** by the [Harvard Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study](https://www.radcliffe.harvard.edu/). In 2017, Lead-DBS was awarded the **Robert-Koch-Prize** of Charité, Berlin.

At the time of writing, [Lead-DBS](http://lead-dbs.org/) was [**downloaded over 65,000 times**](http://www.lead-dbs.org/?page_id=23) by researchers world-wide and currently, [**groups from over 20 institutions contributed**](http://www.lead-dbs.org/?page_id=21) to [Lead-DBS](http://lead-dbs.org/) code & content.

## Lead-DBS methods

Since 2012, we introduced a number of novel methods to analyze DBS recording sites and brain connectivity. As such, the [subcortical electrophysiology mapping](http://www.lead-dbs.org/?page_id=2403) (SEM) approach was introduced in [Horn et al. 2017a](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/hbm.23594/abstract) and applied in [Neumann et al. 2017](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ana.25095/abstract). A summary of the approach can [be found here](http://www.lead-dbs.org/?page_id=2403). A novel method to convert AC/PC coordinates into MNI standard space was introduced in [Horn et al. 2017b](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/28163141) and is [described here](http://www.lead-dbs.org/?page_id=2500). Finally, a method to predict clinical outcome of DBS patients based on brain connectivity – termed Connectivity Benefit Mapping – was introduced in [Horn et al. 2017c](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ana.24974/abstract).
