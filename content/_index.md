<div class="columns"><div class="column column-1-2"><h2>DEAR READER,</h2>


<p>Welcome to the website of the Institute for Network Stimulation – which is based at the University Hospital Cologne.</p>
<p>We are a scientific group with interest in <strong>neuroimaging</strong>, <strong>methods development</strong>, <strong>movement disorders</strong> and <strong>invasive brain stimulation</strong>.</p>
<p>The goal of our research is to <strong>analyse and modulate brain networks</strong> to <strong>improve clinical treatment</strong> in neurological and psychiatric brain disorders. </p>
<p>The primary tools we use to pursue these goals are <strong>structural imaging </strong>and <strong>noninvasive connectivity measures</strong> derived from functional and diffusion weighted MRI.</p>

<div class="columns"><div class="column column-1-3"><div class="image-block"><netstim-image data='{"src":"media/45bea7941215-mesh_lead-1.png","original":"https://www.netstim.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/mesh_lead-1-554x1030.png","alt":"","width":554}'/></div></div><div class="column column-2-3"><h2>Scientific focus</h2>

<p>As a methodological “backbone” of our work, together with others, we develop an open-source software called <a href="http://www.lead-dbs.org"><strong>Lead-DBS</strong></a>.<br/>
Lead-DBS is a toolbox facilitating Deep Brain Stimulation electrode reconstructions and computer simulations based on postoperative MRI &amp; CT imaging.</p>


<div class="action-row"><a aria-label="Visit Lead-DBS.org" class="action external" href="http://www.lead-dbs.org" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Visit Lead-DBS.org</a></div>

<div class="action-row"><a aria-label="Learn more about our work on Deep Brain Stimulation" class="action" href="@root@research-interests/deep-brain-stimulation/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Learn more about our work on Deep Brain Stimulation</a></div>

<p>Further scientific contributions include estimating and validating <a href="http://www.netstim.berlin/research-interests/normative-connectomes/">normative brain connectomes</a> – i.e. “average wiring diagrams” of the human brain. These atlases describe which area is how strongly connected to which other areas of the brain. Over the years, we have created several – they are all freely available to the scientific community and <a href="http://andreas-horn.de/?page_id=30">were applied in a context where subject- or patient-specific connectograms are not available</a>.</p>
