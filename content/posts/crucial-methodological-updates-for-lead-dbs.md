<article class="post-item"><div class="post-image"><a aria-label="1-s2.0-S221315821830305X-gr1-1" href="@root@wp-content/uploads/2018/11/1-s2.0-S221315821830305X-gr1-1.jpg" title="1-s2.0-S221315821830305X-gr1-1"><netstim-image data='{"src":"media/7b45b0b6633f-1-s2.0-S221315821830305X-gr1-1.jpg","original":"https://www.netstim.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/1-s2.0-S221315821830305X-gr1-1.jpg","alt":"","width":625}'/></a></div><div class="standard-content"><header aria-label="Post: Important methodological updates for Lead-DBS"><h2>Important methodological updates for Lead-DBS</h2><span class="post-meta"><time datetime="2018-11-04T18:05:24+00:00">November 4, 2018</time>/<a href="@root@crucial-methodological-updates-for-lead-dbs/#respond">0 Comments</a>/in <a href="@root@category/uncategorized/">Uncategorized</a>/by <a href="@root@author/andreashorn/">andreashorn</a></span></header><p>We are thrilled that the <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327380509_Lead-DBS_v2_Towards_a_comprehensive_pipeline_for_deep_brain_stimulation_imaging">Lead-DBS v2 methodological paper</a> is out in NeuroImage.</p>
<p>[An unpaywalled preprint can be found <a href="https://www.biorxiv.org/content/early/2018/08/28/322008">here</a>].</p>

<p>Lead-DBS was developed since 2012 and the first methods paper published in 2014. Since then, nearly everything has changed – and this led us to writing up the aforementioned updated methods paper. In the manuscript, we describe tools for multispectral nonlinear registration, structural/functional connectivity analyses, brain shift correction, reconstruction of microelectrode recordings and orientation detection of segmented DBS leads. The long author list reflects how many people have contributed to Lead-DBS development since then.</p>

<p>One particularly important processing step is nonlinear registration (patient brains to an atlas or the other way around). Currently, ten approaches to do so are readily built into Lead-DBS and each comes with adjustable settings. Thus, it became very important to investigate and quantify results that may originate from those different approaches. In a marathon effort spearheaded by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Siobhan_Ewert">Siobhan Ewert</a> and led by <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Todd_Herrington">Todd M Herrington</a> that involved &gt;11,000 nonlinear warps, the two most promising normalization approaches were identified – ANTs based multispectral SyN registration as well as multispectram SPM-based segmentation – which are now the default choices in Lead-DBS. In fact, automatic segmentations of the STN/GPi performed using those approaches were marginally if not at all significantly worse than manual expert segmentations.</p>
<p>Their article <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327908494_Optimization_and_comparative_evaluation_of_nonlinear_deformation_algorithms_for_atlas-based_segmentation_of_DBS_target_nuclei">can be found here</a>.</p>


<p>Finally, a new study out of Bern/Oxford validated the default STN atlas defined in Lead-DBS (<a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317032527_Toward_defining_deep_brain_stimulation_targets_in_MNI_space_A_subcortical_atlas_based_on_multimodal_MRI_histology_and_structural_connectivity">Siobhan Ewert’s DISTAL atlas</a>) using microelectrode recordings. This important work showed that anatomical and electrophysiological accuracy was highest for the DISTAL atlas when comparing it to two different atlases of the STN. <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327921854_Accuracy_of_different_three-dimensional_subcortical_human_brain_atlases_for_DBS_-lead_localisation">The article can be found here</a>.</p>
