<p>Thank you very much for your interest in our group.</p>
<p>Our research focusses on the following central questions:</p>
<ul>
<li>How does the human brain respond to focal stimuli applied by invasive and noninvasive neuromodulation methods
<ul>
<li>Specifically, we study the effects of deep brain stimulation on small brain nuclei</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>How do distributed brain networks respond to such stimuli
<ul>
<li>We use noninvasive techniques like resting-state fMRI and diffusion-weighted MRI to address this question</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>How does DBS electrode placement affect
<ul>
<li>Clinical</li>
<li>Neural</li>
<li>or behavioral response</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p>In second line, we’re highly interested in structural and functional connectomics as well as detailed anatomical definitions of the human brain.</p>

<h2>What you can learn at the netstim</h2>
<p>Nearly all of our research projects are tightly intertwined with building and maintaining the scientific open-source toolbox “Lead-DBS” (<a href="http://www.lead-dbs.org">www.lead-dbs.org</a>). Thus, we expect that each member of our lab contributes to Lead-DBS in some way. Contributions could be in form of code and code-development but also documentation, testing and teaching (e.g. in one of our <a href="http://www.lead-dbs.org/workshops/">Lead-DBS workshops</a>). Thus, besides scientific work, the participation in an open-source software project is something you can experience here. Scientifically, projects focus on how focal stimulation of the brain result in distributed network changes throughout the brain and how such changes relate to behavioral, clinical and electrophysiological responses. To investigate these relationships, we often apply MRI based neuroimaging techniques (such as diffusion-weighted MRI based tractography, resting-state functional MRI, structural MRI and simulations). Why not browse to our <a href="@root@about-us-2/research-interests/">research interests</a> or previous <a href="@root@about-us-2/publications/">publications</a> to get a better picture?</p>
